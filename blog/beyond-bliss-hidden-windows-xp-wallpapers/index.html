Beyond Bliss: The Hidden Stories Behind Windows XP Wallpapers

Published: November 2026 • By onlinegames.stream Editorial Archive • 6 min read

Almost everyone on Earth recognizes Bliss: the iconic photograph of emerald rolling hills and cerulean skies taken in Sonoma County, California by Charles O'Rear in 1996. It became the default desktop background for hundreds of millions of computers running Windows XP.

The Forgotten Companion Wallpapers

While Bliss took all the spotlight, Windows XP shipped with an extraordinary library of deeply atmospheric, dreamlike photography that defined early-2000s desktop aesthetics:

  • Autumn: A moody, rain-soaked road framed by gold and crimson foliage in Burlington, Vermont.
  • Azul: A hyper-saturated minimalist seascape that felt like looking out onto the edge of infinity.
  • Red Moon Desert: A mysterious crimson twilight over rippling desert sand dunes that felt entirely otherworldly.
  • Wind: A surreal composition of blowing grass against an azure sky that captured the breeze itself.
